Project Overview

✨ About Tamil Nadu Agricultural University

Tamil Nadu Agricultural University (TNAU) is based in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu. They have over two decades of research data on Indian agriculture. TNAU wants farmers to utilize this information effectively. They wanted a system where they can reach out to farmers and provide them with the best quality seeds, fertilizers, various services like renting an agriculture drone, soil testing, and provide information on loans, government services, etc.

🛒 An e-commerce system that could adapt to how they operate and provide the best quality seeds from them or through their vendors.

TNAU was using Zoho Commerce for its e-commerce business. They offer a variety of products like crop seeds (paddy, maize, pulses, cotton, turmeric), vegetable seeds, crop boosters, bio-fertilizer, micronutrients, organic inputs, and water-soluble fertilizers.

Problem Space

⚠️ Why was Revamp Needed?

Around 26.83% of total orders were being canceled every month. From May 2023 to February 2024, the monthly cancellation rate averaged 26.83%, resulting in a monthly loss of ₹17,234. While this financial loss might seem small, the main concern was the impact on customer trust.

It's already a big deal to get farmers to buy online, and one bad piece of feedback can spread very badly through word of mouth. Farmers lose trust in the digital seed sourcing system if their orders fail silently after placement.

💡 Executive Insight: The Cost of Manual Verification

Manual phone verification post-checkout created an unsustainable bottleneck. Automating order routing directly to vendor self-service portals was critical to preserving farmer trust and preventing order drop-offs.

Discovery

💡 Researching the Problem Space

We conducted extensive field research, interviewing university staff, operations managers, agricultural experts, and vendors at TNAU Coimbatore. The research was divided into two methodologies:

  • Virtual & In-person Interviews with operation teams, agricultural product experts, and vendors.
  • On-site visits to Tamil Nadu Agriculture University, Coimbatore, to observe daily fulfillment workflows.

Research Findings

⚠️ Problems / Pain-Points / Answers to Research Questions (11 Cards)

During the research with operations managers, vendors, and agricultural experts, we gathered critical information across 11 key operational dimensions:

1. How many staff operate the operation?
Currently, there are 2-3 members who operate e-commerce, and it is a very manual process. They have to manually track the inventory by connecting with the vendor. When a new order is received, the team contacts the vendor and then accepts the order.
2. Who decides the product and pricing?
TNAU itself decides the product list and pricing. They are connected to several FPOs and vendors who are their partners.
3. Sourcing to delivery process?
TNAU has partnered with several FPOs and vendors. When an order is received, the admin team manually checks the order, calls the vendor to confirm, and then accepts or rejects the order.
4. Most used vs least used features?
The operations team focuses primarily on order management. Very rarely does anyone from the team add, delete, or update products or manage inventory.
5. What are their priorities while working?
Orders arrive via email/phone. Fulfilling occurs during office hours, but team members are university staff busy with other academic work. The 24-hour SLA is often delayed due to college priorities or holidays, causing cancellations.
6. Overall use & operation time?
The operation team generally fulfills orders within 24 hours when present. Easy to navigate order management, but staff frequently have to cancel orders due to stock delays.
7. How does TNAU deal with vendors?
TNAU has 60-70 vendors across different Tamil Nadu districts (e.g., vendor A for paddy, vendor B for cotton). TNAU also holds central stock in university warehouses procured from FPOs.
8. How do vendors operate?
Vendors receive a manual phone call from TNAU to confirm product & quantity. Customer addresses are shared manually by TNAU staff, and vendor payouts are handled privately.
9. Difficulties faced while operating?
Apart from order management, most sections in Zoho Commerce are unused. Zoho Commerce was built for single merchants and failed to meet multi-vendor aggregator requirements.
10. Desired improvements & feature wishlist
  • Online vendor self-service store portal.
  • Product combo bundling (seeds + bio-boosters).
  • Services & drone rental booking appointments.
  • Soil testing (pH testing) appointment feature.
  • Online vendor account management & payouts.
  • Product variant management.
  • GST billing & customized invoice generation.
11. Complaints faced from customers?
Farmers complain about non-delivery, uncommunicated cancellations, and zero response. Farmers waiting for sowing season suffer crop loss when orders cancel silently days later.

Analysis

🔍 Key Insights & Card Sorting

TNAU collaborates with 60 to 70 vendors (FPOs, seed centers, research stations) selling seeds, agricultural inputs, equipment, and services. Existing off-the-shelf software (Zoho Commerce) was built for single-merchant operations and could not handle multi-vendor order routing or vendor-specific inventory management.

Strategy

🎯 Architecture Model: Org-Branch-POS

We structured the application around a multi-tier organizational model:

🏢 Organization (Merchant) = TNAU Central Admin
🏪 Branch = Vendor Portal (FPOs, Seed Centers)
🛒 POS = Physical Offline Outlets & Kiosks

This hierarchy allows TNAU to maintain central product catalogs and pricing governance while delegating stock updates, order acceptance, and shipping dispatches directly to individual vendor branches.

System Design

📍 Product Architecture & Navigation

We mapped out navigation paths for both TNAU Central Super Admin and Vendor Branch Managers to ensure seamless switching between order views, inventory management, customer support, and vendor payouts.

Core Redesign

📦 Multi-Vendor Order Management Workflow

When a customer places an order containing items from multiple vendors, the platform automatically splits the order into sub-orders and routes them directly to respective vendor portals. Vendors accept or reject sub-orders within a strict 12-hour SLA.

Validation

📢 Stakeholder & Operation Team Feedback

We reviewed early prototypes with university officials and operations personnel to confirm operational feasibility:

✅ Automated Vendor Accept/Reject: Replaced phone calls with direct vendor portal notifications and sub-order reassignment logic.

✅ Automated Courier Tracking Sync: API integration with logistics carriers automatically transitions order statuses to "Shipped" and "Delivered".

Usability

🔥 Usability Testing with Maze & Heatmaps

We ran 50+ Maze usability tests alongside in-person interviews with non-technical operations staff to test task completion rates and navigation clarity.

Iterative Design

📢 Usability Iterations & Interface Refinements

Testing revealed that active buttons during initial order placement confused non-technical staff. We updated the UI to disable non-applicable action triggers until prerequisite confirmation steps were completed.

Before: Action options confused users during order placed stage
After: Non-applicable actions disabled during confirmation

Recognition

📰 Public News & Media Coverage

The revamped platform received widespread media recognition across Tamil Nadu press and government agricultural news for empowering farmers with reliable digital seed procurement.

Reflection

📚 Key Takeaways

Designing for large government organizations requires patience, deep empathy for operational constraints, and clear visual alignment with non-technical stakeholders. By shifting from manual phone verification to automated vendor routing, TNAU transformed its online marketplace into a high-scale digital asset for the agricultural community.

Problem Space

The Bottlenecks of Manual Operations

  • 26.83% Cancellation Rate: Staff manually phoned vendors post-checkout, causing high order drop-offs.
  • 24-Hour Approval SLA: University staff overloaded with academic tasks, causing missed fulfillment windows.

System Architecture

Organization-Branch-POS Multi-Vendor Model

  • Org (Merchant): TNAU Central Admin maintains product setup, catalog pricing, and master governance.
  • Branch (Vendors): Direct self-serve portal for vendors to manage inventory and confirm shipments.
  • POS (Outlets): Integration for physical retail outlets selling seeds and agricultural inputs.
💡 Breakthrough: Replaced single-merchant software with scalable multi-vendor infrastructure.
Core Product Feature

Automated Order & Vendor Workflow

  • Automated SLA Triggers: Vendors receive instant sub-order notifications with automated accept/reject timelines.
  • Merchant Re-assignment: Rejected sub-orders automatically flag admin dashboard for seamless vendor reassignment.
  • Live Courier Sync: Real-time carrier tracking auto-updates status to "Shipped" and "Delivered".
✅ Outcome: Eliminated daily phone calls between TNAU staff and vendors.
Validation & Usability

50+ Prototype Tests via Maze
