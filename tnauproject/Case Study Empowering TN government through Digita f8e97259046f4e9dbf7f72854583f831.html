Case Study: Empowering TN government through Digital Solutions

🚀 Post-Project Impact

📈  Increase in avg. monthly sales of ₹62,826/month to ₹77,01,486/month

💡 Vendor Base Soars 15x — From 70 to 1,000 and Counting!

Sales have seen a dramatic increase over the past few years. From 2023 to 2024, sales grew by 91%, increasing from an average of ₹62,826 per month to ₹1,20,000 per month. This initial growth was then completely overshadowed by a monumental leap in 2025, where the implementation of new systems resulted in average monthly sales of ₹77,01,486. This represents an astonishing 6,317.90% growth from the previous year, highlighting how the successful integration of technology can exponentially enhance revenue streams and drive business success.

✨ Project Overview

🌱 Tamil Nadu Agricultural University (TNAU) is based in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu. They have over two decades (or 20 years) of research data on Indian agriculture. TNAU wants farmers to utilize this information effectively. TNAU wanted a system where they can reach out to farmers and provide them with the best quality seeds, fertilizers, various services like renting an agriculture drone, soil testing, and provide information on loans, government services, etc.

To achieve this, first, they wanted to have

🛒 An e-commerce system that could adapt to how they operate and provide the best quality seeds from them or through their vendors.

TNAU was using Zoho Commerce for its e-commerce business via its website TNAUagricart.com. They offer a variety of products like crop seeds (paddy, maize, pulses, cotton, turmeric), vegetable seeds in packets, crop boosters, bio-fertilizer, micronutrients, organic inputs, and water-soluble fertilizers. Additionally, they provide drone rental services and soil tests like pH tests and other related analyses.

While TNAU was using Zoho Commerce, they were facing a wide variety of issues in managing its e-commerce operations. Faced a lot of negative reviews from customers and stakeholders.

💡 Researching Problem Space

Before diving into researching and talking to stakeholders we did desk research of the current ecommerce system of TNAU.

We talked to operation users, customers, and ran analyses to really understand the pain points. For our e‑commerce setup, this showed us just how much the manual, single‑vendor system was causing late shipments, missed orders, and endless customer follow‑ups. With those insights, we aimed to build a multi‑vendor‑friendly system that automates order handling — meaning fewer cancellations and happier customers.

👥 User Interview with Operations Managers

To gather in‑depth insights on e‑commerce order management challenges — especially issues caused by a manual, single‑vendor system — and to understand customer preferences for a smoother, multi‑vendor shopping experience, through focused discussions with a select group of users.

⚠️ Problems / Pain-Points / Answers to questions

How many staff operate the operation?

Currently, there are 2-3 members who operate e-commerce, and it is a very manual process. They have to manually track the inventory by connecting with the vendor. When a new order is received, the team contacts the vendor and then accepts the order.

What is the basic process from sourcing a product to delivering it to the customer?

TNAU has partnered with several FPOs and vendors. They source various products from them. When an order is received, the admin team manually checks the order, calls the vendor to confirm, and then accepts or rejects the order.

What are their priorities while working?

The team receives an alert for new orders via email or phone. They only fulfill orders during office hours. Work generally starts late at the college. The people working in operations are university staff who are also busy with other university work. TNAU has a rule to accept or reject an order within 24 hours. However, this process can be delayed due to university priorities, holidays, or personal work, which has resulted in several missed orders and customer cancellations.

How does TNAU deal with vendors?

TNAU has 60-70 vendors, and each vendor has multiple stores.

TNAU has partnered with vendors who can provide high-quality seeds, crop inputs, etc.

Some vendors have offline stores. All products are in different districts in Tamil Nadu, which generally increases over time. For example, TNAU has partnered with one specific vendor for paddy seeds, another vendor for cotton seeds, etc. TNAU also keeps stocks of several products in the university, which they procure from different vendors or FPOs.

Are there any complaints they faced from customers?

Customers mostly complain about non-delivery, no response, and order cancellation. The reason is that the operations team is unable to fulfill the order from the vendor within 24 hours. The order gets cancelled by the customer due to less responsive customer care, as the operations team is busy with other university work. Several orders are not even seen by the team due to the large number of orders.

Who decides the product and pricing?

TNAU itself decides the product list and pricing. They are connected to several FPOs and vendors who are their partners.

What part do they use the most in the product, and what do they use less?

The operations team focuses primarily on order management. Very rarely does anyone from the team add, delete, or update products or manage inventory.

What is their overall use of the system and the time they take to do the operation?

The operation team has been trained to use the product, as they only handle the order management, so it's easy for them to use. They generally fulfill the order in 24 hours. Easy to navigate the order management page; sometimes, they have to cancel the order.

How do vendors operate?

TNAU has over 60 vendors, a number that could increase over time. They receive a call from TNAU to confirm the product and quantity. The address is shared with them manually by TNAU, and the payment is from TNAU. The vendor payment relationship is handled privately by TNAU.

Difficulties they faced while operating?

Apart from order management, most of the sections in the product are very rarely used. The team mostly had issues with the order management, and their requirement could not able to be met by the current system.

Desired improvements/new features, if any

  • There is no place to manage a vendor's store online.
  • They want to sell a combo of different products.
  • They want to sell services like renting an agricultural drone, soil testing, pH, etc.
  • Appointment Feature for service
  • TNAU wants to handle vendors online; currently, everything is handled on paper.
  • In creating a product, they want to sell different variants of the product.
  • Options to include Vouchers, GST Billing, Customized Invoices.

What are the issues faced when fulfilling orders?

For every order, the team has to call the vendor to check the product's available quantity. Sometimes vendors provide false information, or misunderstandings occur between the team and vendors, leading to order cancellations. Daily arguments with vendors and a lack of accountability on their part have led to a poor vendor relationship. This is the major problem they wanted to solve.

😫 Customer Impact

Due to these issues, the business has developed a bad reputation among customers. When an order is accepted, farmers who are waiting for seeds to sow may have their order canceled a few days later without their knowledge. Many customers get frustrated because their crops might not receive seeds, fertilizer, or insecticides on time. Customers often argue with the TNAU team for not informing them about the order cancellation, the reason being that the TNAU team themselves do not know the status of the orders.

⚠️ Manual Operations and Confusion

The status of every order is changed manually by calling the vendor (e.g., "order accepted," "shipped," or "delivered"). Many times, even after an order has shipped, the team forgets to change the status, leading to a lot of confusion. For example, many products that were delivered last month still had a status of "Order accepted."

🤖 The Need for a New System

They wanted a system where several things were automated and where vendors could be held accountable. This would help reduce poor relationships and, in turn, be better for the business.



🔍 Key Findings from Researching with Clients

📁 Vendors were FPOs and farmers

TNAU has 60-70 vendors, the number of which can increase over time. These vendors sold mainly Seeds, Agri Inputs, Agri Inputs, Produce & Planting Materials, Services, and farm equipment.

🍎 Fixed Vendors for some products or categories

Some vendors were fixed for certain important products to maintain high-quality yield; for example, Maize seeds came from Vendor A, and paddy seeds came from Vendor B.

🛒 Major flaw in doing operations

The current system of doing e-commerce business in Zoho e-commerce is not compatible with TNAU's needs, as the current system is only compatible for an individual selling products online. TNAU wanted to source the product from different places and sell it, being an aggregator.

❌  No Customization with the current tool
There were no customizations available with Zoho e-commerce that could suit with clients’ requirements.

📦 Critical issues with Order Cancellation

A lot of manual work was required because for every order, the team had to call from a list of over 60 vendors to confirm quantities and place the order.

  • Vendors made false promises about product availability.
  • There were frequent arguments with vendors over unfulfilled quantities. Their reasons varied, from an inability to procure products on time to other business issues.
  • Vendors were not held accountable for their mistakes.
  • Simple tasks required an excessive number of phone calls.
  • The operations team often failed to update the order status, which led to incorrect reporting of overall sales.
  • Customer orders included a mix of products that were shipped from different vendors. In the past, there were fewer vendors whose warehouses were nearby, so shipping wasn't a major issue. There were instances where the unavailability of a single, inexpensive product led to the cancellation of an entire order.

🏬 Merchant User Flow

Objective: Manage products, vendors, and orders. The merchant acts as a central hub.

  • Product & Vendor Management: The merchant adds new products to the platform, setting prices and associating them with specific vendors. They also onboard new vendors. 🤝
  • Order Oversight: 📊 The merchant has a dashboard that displays all orders, regardless of status or vendor. They can monitor the full order lifecycle. ⏳
  • Order Rejection & Re-assignment: 🚨 A vendor rejects a sub-order. The status of that specific sub-order is flagged for the merchant. The merchant's dashboard alerts them of the rejected order. 🔄 The merchant manually searches for an alternative vendor and re-assigns the sub-order to them. The new vendor receives a notification. 🔔

📦 Vendor User Flow

Objective: Accept, modify, and fulfill sub-orders assigned to them.

  • New Order Notification: 🔔 The vendor receives a notification (via email or dashboard alert) for a new sub-order. The sub-order's status is "Order Placed."
  • Order Review: 📝 The vendor logs in and reviews the sub-order details, including products and quantities.
  • Accept, Modify, or Reject:
    • Accept: ✅ If the vendor can fulfill the order as is, they accept it. The status of the sub-order changes to "Order Confirmed."
    • Modify: ✏️ If the vendor needs to adjust the quantity (e.g., they're out of a certain product), they can decrease the quantity. The customer and merchant are notified of the change. The sub-order status changes to "Order Confirmed."
    • Reject: ❌ If the vendor cannot fulfill the order at all, they reject it. The sub-order status is flagged for the merchant to reassign. 🚩
  • Packing & Shipping: For a confirmed sub-order, the vendor packs the products.
  • Add Shipping Information: 🚚 The vendor enters the shipping carrier and tracking number into the platform. This action automatically changes the sub-order status to "Order Shipped." The customer receives an email with the tracking information. 📧
  • Delivery Confirmation: 🎉 Once the carrier's tracking information indicates the sub-order has been delivered, the system automatically updates the status to "Order Delivered."

📢 Feedback for New Order Management process

The client was happy with the process that matched their workflow, which helped to improve several issues they were facing.


Solved these important issues:

✅  Staff manually calls vendor for stock; wrong/false stock info, accepting order
Now merchant/TNAU does not need to call the vendor each time to accept or reject the order. The vendor can make the decision themselves, and will be responsible for the customer not getting the order on time.

✅  Some orders get missed due to workload; no auto-confirmation
As vendors were now accountable, most of the things were managed by the vendors themselves; only a few order concerns got to TNAU/merchant.

✅  Delays, miscommunication, arguments, false confirmations
The vendor can keep the inventory of the product available to them, thereby avoiding false promises, delays, and miscommunication with TNAU/merchant.

✅  Time wasted on repetitive calls; lack of accountability
As vendor were handling their orders themselves, there were no calls related to orders between TNAU and vendors, thereby saving time, and vendors were accountable for their orders that came for them.

✅  Wrong status updates; manual calls needed, Often delays or missed updates; status wrong in system
The work done by the vendor changes the status automatically in the system. If vendor accept the order the status changes from “Order Placed” to “Order Confirmed”, once the vendor pack the item and ship it, they need to add the shipping information, when filling it, it automatically changes the status to “Order Shippped”, depending on the shipping company the order can be marked as “Order Delivered” through manually or API integration from logistic provider.

✅  Staff forgets to manually update the delivery status
Now TNAU/merchant knows their role of work, so they do not need to worry about the delivery status, as the vendor can change the status themselves.
